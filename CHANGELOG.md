# Changelog

All notable changes to Tesel. The format follows [Keep a Changelog](https://keepachangelog.com/en/1.1.0/) and versions follow [Semantic Versioning](https://semver.org/). Protocol versions are separate: see [`docs/protocol.md`](docs/protocol.md) §9.

## [0.1.0] - Unreleased

The first version: a shared map for a group, over the internet and over MeshCore or Meshtastic LoRa radios, with the web app as the only client ([ADR-0016](docs/decisions/0016-web-app-and-native-shells.md)).

### Map

- OpenStreetMap vector base map, installable as an app and usable offline.
- This device on the map where it is now, with its accuracy, and a button that keeps the map centred on it. The GPS runs only while something uses it.
- Coordinates in decimal degrees, degrees-minutes-seconds, UTM and MGRS ([ADR-0021](docs/decisions/0021-coordinate-formats.md)): a long press on the map shows a point's coordinates, ready to copy, and coordinates typed as they were dictated are found on the map. Markers and circles show theirs.
- A circle is at most 1 000 km in radius, and none breaks the map: a larger one is refused when sent and discarded when received, and near a pole a circle's ring and bounds stop at the pole, where MapLibre threw for every member of the group (protocol §3.7, [ADR-0105](docs/decisions/0105-circles-stay-on-the-earth.md)).
- Distance and bearing from this device to any point, marker, circle or member, while the app may use the location, and guidance to it: a card with the distance, the bearing and an arrow that points the way on the map, and a line to follow. The target moves with a member and the guidance ends if it leaves the map.
- An "Elevation" fold under a point's distance: opened, the profile of the straight way there drawn to scale, with round heights marked, and metres up, metres down and the steepest stretch; closed, the metres up and down. It needs no relief switched on ([ADR-0115](docs/decisions/0115-elevation-profile-of-a-point.md)).
- A measuring tool for distances along a path and the area it encloses; lines, areas and circles show their length, area or radius.
- Members on the map can be tapped for who they are, their last position and the way there.
- Trails: where each member has been in the last hours, drawn from the positions they share. Hiding one's position hides the trail up to that moment too.
- Map layers: members, trails, markers and zones, tasks, services and weather warnings can each be hidden; the choice is remembered. The panel shows every layer as a tile, lit while it is shown, so what is on is seen at a glance, and the map's credits in one line of small print ([ADR-0071](docs/decisions/0071-panels-read-whole.md)). This device and SOS calls are always shown.
- Trails show where members went, not where the GPS wandered: a change of position counts as a move only beyond the uncertainty of both fixes; a device standing still is published where it was, and its heartbeat repeats that place; trails and swept strips leave out poor fixes and the wandering within the error, for every member and for what is already stored ([ADR-0092](docs/decisions/0092-moves-not-gps-wandering.md)).
- A phone left still indoors no longer draws a tangle: its fix jumps further than the accuracy it claims, so a change counts as a move only once what follows bears it out, the device going on the same way or staying away a minute. Jumps that come back are neither published nor drawn, in trails already stored too ([ADR-0119](docs/decisions/0119-moves-borne-out.md)).
- What reaches this device from each member, and how: a member's details say when something of theirs last arrived by radio or through the server, and the members list marks those whose news arrives by radio.
- Keep the screen on, as an option, and always while guiding to a target.
- Full screen in a browser tab, without the browser's bars, where the browser allows it (Android and computers): on by default from the first tap of each visit, and switched off in the settings.
- The language, Spanish or English, is the browser's until chosen in the settings, and is remembered; the top bar no longer holds it.
- The operation diary: everything that happened in the group, in order and in plain words, downloadable as text for the report ([ADR-0022](docs/decisions/0022-log-files.md)).
- The debrief of an operation, for whoever coordinates: in the diary, «Informe» downloads as a PDF what happened between two times, filled in with the group's last spell of activity: key figures, a map of each person's way with the calls for help, markers and zones, bars of activity, each call for help with who received it and how soon, the tasks, the people, the field reports and the timeline ([ADR-0118](docs/decisions/0118-debrief-of-an-operation.md)).
- The group's log can be saved to a file, encrypted for the group, and loaded on another device of the group to bring it what it is missing (protocol §10).
- A simpler screen: the SOS button and a dock — Me (where you are and how you share your position), Add (everything new: a marker, a route, a zone, a task or a report), Chat (with how many messages from others are unread), Tasks and More ([ADR-0070](docs/decisions/0070-a-simpler-app.md)). Everything else starts from what is touched on the map: a point, a marker or a member opens what can be done there (guide, mark, add a task). The map's controls are one ink group, like the dock: a needle that puts north back up, this device and the map layers; zoom is by pinching. The map's credits show in small print when it opens and stay in the layers panel; the scale sits under the map's controls ([ADR-0039](docs/decisions/0039-map-credits-and-scale.md)).
- One look throughout: every corner, shadow and size of text comes from a few shared values, the chrome around the map (top bar, dock, map controls) is ink and what it shows sits on cards, and map lines and labels turn pale in the dark theme. Points placed on the map, by drawing, measuring or a long press, are fine targets with a dot on the exact spot.
- Straight to the point: Me opens on the person's name, which a pencil changes and the group learns at once; the LoRa radio is one short line ("Linked", or "Not linked" and "Connect"); the top bar shows the group in use under the word "Group", and the connection on the right; a report shows only what happens, its casualties folded with the rest; the diary has no introduction, its log folded below. Chips, two- or three-part choices, folds and forms share one look everywhere ([ADR-0078](docs/decisions/0078-one-look-and-straight-to-the-point.md)).
- Told without having to look: a card on the map tells a member the group cannot see them, with one button to share their position; the members list says in words who is not on the map; sending an SOS closes any panel so the call is in view; another member's SOS says how far it is and guides there; and whoever coordinates is told when someone joins, of each report from another member and when a task is started or finished ([ADR-0080](docs/decisions/0080-told-without-looking.md)). Folded parts of a form no longer close at each change inside them.
- Found in a tap or two: a teammate's card begins with how far they are and "Guide me", and the members list opens each card; the map opening wide frames the operation areas; the drawing card's buttons have names; notices say what was sent, are not repeated, and open what they announce with "View", and a chat line from someone else shows while the chat is closed; tasks are listed by whom they are for, only one's own and those for anyone can be moved along from the list, a task along a route or strip starts by following it, and a task marked done can be undone from its notice ([ADR-0081](docs/decisions/0081-found-in-a-tap-or-two.md)).
- Mistakes easy to correct, and less noise: markers, zones and routes can be edited after saving; deleting waits in the "⋯" menu; no notice at first launch that the app works offline; a new group's server says only "Tesel's"; the offline maps panel shows the size and "Download" first, its options folded and in plain words; an invitation opened at first launch joins in the same step; the radio card names Bluefy for the iPhone and shows an unused radio in grey; the check-in counts minutes; drawings count points plainly; typed coordinates show a decimal example; a search area is not drawn as already cleared; and sharing an invitation comes first ([ADR-0082](docs/decisions/0082-correct-and-quiet.md)).
- No practice mode and no user guide: the app is to explain itself, screen by screen ([ADR-0083](docs/decisions/0083-help-on-one-page.md), [ADR-0086](docs/decisions/0086-no-guide.md)).
- The app explains itself where it is used: after the name, the groups panel opens on the ways into a group; the server folds under «Opciones avanzadas»; inviting shows the QR first and a switch to invite a coordinator; a tap on the map shows that point (the press and hold is gone); this device's dot reads «Tú»; the connection says «En línea», «Por radio» or «Sin conexión»; empty places say what they are for, and each option of «Añadir» has a line; plainer words throughout; an SOS's kind is a tile; Me puts status, position and tasks first and folds the rest; the layers come in two groups and are ticked when on ([ADR-0087](docs/decisions/0087-explained-where-used.md)).
- A refined logo: tighter tiles that weigh as much as the word, in a bolder Archivo.
- Short panels, made to read at a glance: the base map is chosen by its picture and layers are switched on and off; Me says how precise the GPS is (good, fair or poor); the coordinate format is chosen in the settings; "Screen always on" is a switch. A point, an object or a member shows first how far it is and what can be done there, and its coordinates last, on one line tapped to copy ([ADR-0070](docs/decisions/0070-a-simpler-app.md)).
- Topographic map and aerial photographs of Spain, Portugal and southern France, as base maps chosen in the map layers panel, and downloadable with an offline area: one Sentinel-2 mosaic alike for every country from afar, Andorra included, and each country's newest orthophotos close up, out to every shore and island (IGN's PNOA, DGT's OrtoSat 2023, IGN France's BD ORTHO), joined where a tile crosses a border or a coast ([ADR-0023](docs/decisions/0023-ign-base-maps.md), [ADR-0041](docs/decisions/0041-satellite-mosaic-and-national-photos.md)). The topographic map is drawn from vector data, sharp at every zoom and with a dark version: the street map in a topographic palette with contour lines worked out on the device from the elevation, their heights, and in Spain IGN's names of mountains, places, ravines, caves and springs ([ADR-0034](docs/decisions/0034-vector-topographic-map.md)).
- Each base map has its own job: the street map, with neutral land, buildings and roads coloured by class, is for finding a street and a way in; the topographic map for the terrain; the satellite map for the ground as it is ([ADR-0035](docs/decisions/0035-base-maps-by-purpose.md)).
- Three base maps: topographic, the base map, satellite and tactical. The street map is gone, and the tactical map is a base map of its own rather than what dark mode turned the street map into; dark mode darkens the app and leaves the map. Every downloaded area holds the topographic map with its contour lines, relief and names. Over any map, fires detected by satellite in the last day or two (NASA GIBS) and the zones a 100-year flood reaches in Spain (SNCZI, drawn through the app's server, protocol §8.3), online only ([ADR-0120](docs/decisions/0120-three-base-maps-and-hazards.md), [ADR-0121](docs/decisions/0121-flood-zones-through-the-server.md)).
- The cadastre, as a layer: parcels with their numbers and, in Spain, the rural polygons, how a plot in the countryside is named, from the cadastre services of Spain and France, one picture of the view at a time and only online, as its terms ask ([ADR-0036](docs/decisions/0036-cadastre-layer.md)).
- Relief, as an option: hills and valleys shaded on any base map, and the climb, descent and steepest stretch of the way to a point, shown with its distance and while guiding, from Mapterhorn's elevation, built from each country's best open model ([ADR-0024](docs/decisions/0024-terrain-tiles-for-relief.md), [ADR-0037](docs/decisions/0037-maps-across-borders.md)).
- The ground in 3D: a button among the map's controls raises hills and valleys, from the same elevation, offline too, shades them, leans the map back to near eye level under a sky with haze on the horizon, and raises them more from afar than up close; it is off whenever the app opens, and a screen at rest lays it flat ([ADR-0102](docs/decisions/0102-ground-in-3d.md), [ADR-0106](docs/decisions/0106-ground-in-3d-that-reads.md)).
- Tasks: a short text, typed or picked from nine common ones (search the area, check a home, treat an injured person…), and, if wanted, who should do it and a place on the map; to do, in progress or done. Anyone can add one from the Tasks button, picking its place on the map from the task itself ("Pick on the map", which keeps what was typed), or with "Task here" on a point or an object, take it, finish it or hand it on. The dock counts this device's tasks and a notice says when someone hands one over; tasks with a place show on the map, and the diary tells how each moved along. A new event type, `TASK`, carries each change in one radio message (protocol §3.11, [ADR-0025](docs/decisions/0025-tasks.md)).
- Public services on the map: health centres and hospitals, pharmacies, fire stations, police, helipads, town halls and fuel stations from the base map, with round icons from zoom 12, a «Servicios» layer switch, and their point with guidance when tapped. They work offline in downloaded areas ([ADR-0027](docs/decisions/0027-public-services-from-the-base-map.md)).
- Official weather warnings: the yellow, orange and red warnings of AEMET and IPMA, gathered by the server from MeteoAlarm, are drawn on the map unlike the group's own areas, with a cloud on a rounded square ([ADR-0038](docs/decisions/0038-official-warnings-look.md)): hatched when seen from afar, and close up only a thin edge with a band fading inwards, so the inside stays clear to work on. A card says what is in force where this device is; closed, it leaves one line at the top of the map that opens it again ([ADR-0100](docs/decisions/0100-warnings-leave-the-map-clear.md)). A long press shows the warnings at any point. They are kept for use without coverage, and have their own layer switch. The server publishes them for any app at `v1/weather-warnings` (protocol §8.2, [ADR-0030](docs/decisions/0030-weather-warnings.md)).
- The simulator's scenarios can tell what members carry, form teams, give and move on tasks, report and sweep; the Turia scenario does.
- The MGRS grid over the map, as a switch in the map layers: lines every 100 km down to 100 m as the zoom allows, with their digits, the 100 km squares named and the edges of UTM zones ([ADR-0021](docs/decisions/0021-coordinate-formats.md)).
- A warning on entering a danger area: each device compares its own position with the group's danger areas and vibrates and shows which one, offline too; the GPS stays on while there is one ([ADR-0033](docs/decisions/0033-danger-area-warning.md)).
- The radar, as a switch in the map layers: rings around this device at round distances that follow the zoom, and dashed lines to the five nearest members with a recent position, each with its distance and bearing. It keeps the GPS on while shown.
- Areas downloaded tile by tile for offline use ([ADR-0011](docs/decisions/0011-client-side-area-download.md)). Each tile is tried again a few times; a download that stopped short can be resumed, skipping what it has, and can be cancelled at any moment. Without any download the map is never blank offline: an overview of the base map is kept, and the tiles last looked at, up to 64 MB. A download first checks there is room, and leaves some for the group's log ([ADR-0056](docs/decisions/0056-storage-and-offline-maps.md)).
- A faster map: each layer redraws only when its own part of the group changes, a tap goes to what is drawn on top, members' names no longer pile up (an SOS always shows), and the map frames this device and the group when it opens on its wide view. The app comes in parts: MapLibre in one of its own, which a new version of the app does not download again, and the satellite map, the QR reader and the radio when first needed, so an update is about a third of what it was ([ADR-0053](docs/decisions/0053-load-heavy-parts-on-demand.md), [ADR-0054](docs/decisions/0054-frame-the-group-at-start.md)).
- Opening a group with a long operation behind it is quicker: positions no trail can show any more are forgotten, and the log stays bounded ([ADR-0055](docs/decisions/0055-forget-outlived-positions.md)).
- The operation area: a coordinator draws an area with the operation area style, and every member is offered its map to download, with its size, in one tap ([ADR-0029](docs/decisions/0029-operation-area.md), protocol §4.2).
- The base map (Spain, Portugal and the islands) is built from OpenStreetMap by our own server and served by it.

### Groups

- Local identity with no accounts: an alias and a key pair generated on the device.
- A name with a broken character, such as half an emoji cut off by the field's length, is refused with that reason, for a person and for a group, whose invitations could not be made.
- Groups with coordinator and member roles; invitations by link or QR code, scanned in any browser.
- Groups in one panel: the group in use with its members, shown by their initials with signs for a live position and for radio, and «Invitar»; for whoever coordinates, teams and the check-in fold under «Organizar»; leaving is at the bottom. With no group, a «Para empezar» card on the map offers creating one or joining one; the server of a new group is proposed and tucked away ([ADR-0070](docs/decisions/0070-a-simpler-app.md)).
- Leaving a group forgets all it left on the device, including what was still being written for it as it was left, cleared the next time the app opens. A group that cannot be opened says so, with a button to try again.
- One binary event format on every transport, end-to-end encrypted with the group secret ([protocol v1](docs/protocol.md)).

### Situational awareness

- Live positions with three privacy modes: sharing, paused and hidden. Hiding holds for every group: one that still shows the position hides it when it is next opened.
- Group chat, with dictation: a microphone in the text box writes what is said, in Spanish, with the phone's speech recognition, to be checked and sent ([ADR-0040](docs/decisions/0040-dictated-messages.md)).
- Markers and areas with a symbol catalogue, editable by any member. Circles from other clients are shown (protocol §3.7).
- Routes: drawn point by point, up to 25, or read from a GPX, KML or GeoJSON file of up to 5 MB and thinned to 25 points, saying how far at most the route strays from the file's. "Follow route" guides to its start and then point by point to its end, with the distance left and a warning on straying more than 50 m from it. A task can be put on a route, or on any object, and follows it; at the end of the route, one tap marks this device's task there done (protocol §3.11, [ADR-0059](docs/decisions/0059-routes.md)).
- Zones read from a KML or GeoJSON file too: its largest polygon, or a track that closes on itself, thinned like a route. A marker, each point of a route or a zone, and a task's place can be typed as coordinates, in any format the app writes, in place of a tap ([ADR-0077](docs/decisions/0077-coordinates-and-zone-files.md)).
- Sweeps: whoever coordinates splits a search area into parallel lanes, as far apart as the terrain allows (open ground 25 m, scrub 10 m, dense forest 5 m, or any spacing), and the lanes into one strip per team, each in its own colour and handed out as a task. The strips show on the map before they are created; an area that would take more than a thousand lanes is refused, so no device has to work them out. "Follow strip" guides along its lanes; each strip says how much of it its walker's trail has swept; the area is marked cleared once every strip is done. A new event type, `SWEEP`, carries only the recipe; every device works out the lanes (protocol §3.13, [ADR-0060](docs/decisions/0060-sweeps.md)).
- A swept area is marked cleared once, and only once the mark is sure to win: a device whose clock was behind that of whoever last changed the area marked it again at every change of the group, without end; it now waits for its clock to pass that change.
- Me and each member's card: status in one tap (active, resting, off duty) first, and everything else in view, without opening anything ([ADR-0071](docs/decisions/0071-panels-read-whole.md)): the profile (civil protection, firefighters, police…), battery, position, equipment (first-aid kit, defibrillator, water, radio set, weapon…) and tasks, the same for oneself and for anyone tapped on the map. Members resting or off duty are drawn as empty rings, and a change of status goes to the diary. Sharing the position is one switch, with a second to hide where one was. A new event type, `MEMBER_STATE`, tells it in 21 bytes, the battery in steps of 10 % (protocol §3.14, [ADR-0061](docs/decisions/0061-member-state.md)).
- Twenty-five things a member can say they carry, in four groups in Me (care and supplies, rescue and safety, tools and power, transport and communication): now also a 4×4, a boat, a search dog, a thermal camera, a satellite phone, a generator, a power bank, a chainsaw, a water pump, hand tools, oxygen and a megaphone, in the same 21 bytes (protocol §3.14, [ADR-0079](docs/decisions/0079-more-equipment-in-groups.md)).
- Who stays in touch without coverage: Me and each member's card say, on a line of their own, whether a LoRa radio is linked to the member's app, and so whether they can still be reached if the mobile network falls; Me leads to linking one. The app tells it by itself, in one more equipment bit of `MEMBER_STATE`, never picked by hand, and the members list marks it with the radio's antenna (protocol §3.14, [ADR-0069](docs/decisions/0069-who-keeps-in-touch-without-coverage.md)).
- Teams: whoever coordinates makes teams in the group panel, under «Organizar», with a name, one of six colours and their members; the members are listed team by team. A task, or a strip of a sweep, can be for a whole team: it counts as each member's, they are told when it is handed, and the strips take the team's colour and count all its members' trails. Members are drawn in their team's colour. A new event type, `TEAM`, carries a team whole (protocol §3.15, [ADR-0062](docs/decisions/0062-teams.md)).
- Situation reports: from Add, a short form in the order emergency services use (METHANE): what is happening, the casualties by triage category, and, folded, the dangers, the access and the help needed, with a note; it goes from where the device is. Reports are pinned in the chat, gathered in a "Reports" tab with the casualties added up, drawn on the map behind their own layer switch and written in the diary. A new event type, `REPORT`, carries one in a radio message (protocol §3.16, [ADR-0063](docs/decisions/0063-situation-reports.md)).
- The “Are you OK?” check: whoever coordinates turns it on in the group panel, under «Organizar»; a member who is active and has not moved or touched the app for 30 minutes is asked, with a vibration, and if no one answers in 2 minutes an SOS goes out with their last position, as from the SOS button. Answering sends nothing, and it never asks someone resting or off duty; time with the app off screen does not count, and a GPS fix wandering on the spot is not a move. A new event type, `CHECK_IN_TIMER`, carries the setting (protocol §3.17, [ADR-0064](docs/decisions/0064-check-in.md)).
- The “Are you OK?” check says an SOS went out only once it has: one that could not be sent shows as such on the card, with a vibration and a tone, and is tried again, after 5 seconds and then twice as long each time up to a minute, until it goes out or the member answers.
- SOS calls with kind, position and acknowledgements; cancellation by the sender or a coordinator. An SOS never waits for the GPS: without a fix it goes at once, says the place is not known yet, and the position follows as soon as there is one (protocol §3.8, [ADR-0043](docs/decisions/0043-radio-delivery.md)).
- The alarm: another member's SOS vibrates and sounds a short tone, and, while the app is in the background, show a notification that brings it back. The tone and the notifications are switches in "More" ([ADR-0047](docs/decisions/0047-alarms-and-notices.md)).
- A new SOS closes the chat, which would cover its card; a chat open on older messages stays there while the group goes on.
- What has not reached anyone yet is marked: own chat messages and SOS calls waiting for a server or the radio say so.
- What a switch does, when its name is not enough, and how a part of the app works (teams, pairing a radio, offline maps, coordinate formats) are shown on tapping the (i) beside its name, and hidden again the same way, so panels take less room; menus and empty screens say no more than a name or a few words ([ADR-0101](docs/decisions/0101-explanations-on-asking.md)).
- Accessible: colours meet WCAG 2.1 AA in both themes (danger red is a little darker on light surfaces, and the dark theme's SOS button is the same red as the light one's), panels and menus work from the keyboard, confirmations use the app's own dialog, texts with a number take its plural, and the side safe areas of phones with a notch are kept clear, and text fields are written at 16 px or more, so that an iPhone does not zoom the page in, cut off at the sides, on tapping one ([ADR-0057](docs/decisions/0057-accessible-interface.md)). Weather warnings name and link their source.
- The app asks the browser to keep its storage from the first identity, and says when the device is out of room, offering to delete offline maps.
- One place for messages to the user, at the top of the map under the incidents, so they never cover a button. A form that could not be sent keeps what was typed and says so; any action that fails says so too; a second window that holds the app's storage is explained, with a button to reload.
- A roomier chat: it fills the screen above the dock, writes in one row (the text with its microphone; Send), runs one person's lines under one name, and shows each report as one line that opens with a tap ([ADR-0065](docs/decisions/0065-room-for-what-is-read.md)).
- Fewer buttons elsewhere too: a panel shows at most three actions, the rest in a "⋯" menu; the "More" menu holds the tools and opens a settings panel with the device's choices; and with a group in use, the other groups and the ways to a new one fold under it. Drawing and measuring show a short card: one line and small buttons ([ADR-0065](docs/decisions/0065-room-for-what-is-read.md)).
- A simpler app: coordinators' alerts and the roll call are gone from the app (the protocol keeps `ALERT` and `ROLL_CALL` for other clients); what is saved or sent says in a short notice whether it reached the group, went by radio or waits for a connection; forms close with «Cancelar» (or «Listo» once done); new markers are picked from a grid of icons with short names; an empty task list says how to add one; and the top bar is slimmer ([ADR-0070](docs/decisions/0070-a-simpler-app.md)).

### Transports

- Internet: a zero-knowledge relay that stores and forwards encrypted envelopes (protocol §8). A connection that dies without closing is noticed within 45 seconds; a batch that cannot be stored is fetched again; envelopes whose event does not match their key are dropped; the same event always seals into the same envelope, so repeats cost the server nothing ([ADR-0067](docs/decisions/0067-envelope-nonce-from-the-event.md)).
- Each device keeps its own events in order even if its clock goes back by up to an hour, and says when its clock differs from the server's by more than two minutes ([ADR-0044](docs/decisions/0044-clocks-and-damaged-events.md)). One event stamped by a clock that ran ahead no longer holds every later event at that time once the clock is set right ([ADR-0103](docs/decisions/0103-rivals-full-storage-and-urgent-sync.md)).
- An event written under another member's next sequence number no longer silences their SOS: of different events under one `(origin, seq)`, every device keeps the same, every SOS, cancellation and acknowledgement among them or else the one whose bytes come first, whatever order they arrive in; the app says someone is sending messages under that member's name, and a device that sees its own name on a number it has not used skips past it (protocol §3.1, [ADR-0103](docs/decisions/0103-rivals-full-storage-and-urgent-sync.md)).
- A full storage no longer keeps SOS calls from the screen or from the group: a write of the log first frees the map tiles seen online and goes again; SOS calls, cancellations and acknowledgements that still find no room reach the picture, the alarm, the server and the radio, held in memory until they can be stored; an own SOS goes out without being stored; and a backlog that cannot be stored no longer keeps the outbox from the server ([ADR-0103](docs/decisions/0103-rivals-full-storage-and-urgent-sync.md)).
- An own SOS, its cancellation and acknowledgements go to the server as soon as the device has joined, ahead of the backlog; a backlog that arrives faster than it is stored is read again in parts instead of piling up in memory ([ADR-0103](docs/decisions/0103-rivals-full-storage-and-urgent-sync.md)).
- MeshCore LoRa through a companion radio over Web Bluetooth (protocol §7), with priorities, a legal airtime budget, position thinning, SOS repeats and bridging between radio and internet by any member who has both ([ADR-0015](docs/decisions/0015-lora-airtime-policy.md)). What was written without a radio goes on the air once one is linked; an SOS repeats until it is acknowledged or cancelled, also after the radio refuses a repeat, and one answered while there was no radio is not sent again; a position never goes out after a newer one or a hiding; a damaged frame never stands in for the event it claims to be; open SOS calls are bridged however old; answers to them go first; a lost Bluetooth link comes back by itself; frames of the device's other groups are kept for them; and leaving a group frees its channel in the radio ([ADR-0043](docs/decisions/0043-radio-delivery.md)).
- The app and the site speak of nodes and the mesh ("Conectar nodo", "por la malla", "Malla pública"), not radios: what Tesel carries without coverage is data over a network of the team's own, not talk over a walkie-talkie ([ADR-0112](docs/decisions/0112-nodes-and-the-mesh.md)).
- Text messages of other MeshCore and Meshtastic apps, such as those on a radio's public channel, are kept, read-only, in the chat's "Malla pública" / "Public mesh" tab.
- Every radio frame is sealed by Tesel, inside the radio's own encryption: AES-256-CTR from a 12-byte synthetic IV, an HMAC-SHA256 of the event, under keys derived from the group secret for the radio alone (protocol §5.2). Nobody without the secret can read, change or make up what goes by radio, on MeshCore (whose ECB and 2-byte MAC were weak) or Meshtastic (which authenticates nothing); a frame that does not open is dropped. The seal takes 12 bytes, so events are at most 153 bytes and texts 12 bytes shorter: 139 for a chat line, 128 for an SOS, 117 for a task; a team holds 25 members and a drawn line or area 22 points. Events of up to 165 bytes written before still open and sync over the internet, but stay off the air. MeshCore frames moved to `data_type` 0xFF4E; devices of earlier versions are not heard by radio ([ADR-0099](docs/decisions/0099-sealed-radio-frames.md)).
- Meshtastic radios, as a second kind beside MeshCore: "Connect radio" lists radios of both kinds and loads only the code of the one picked; the group is a secondary channel of the radio whose key is the group secret, set without touching any other, and events travel to everyone on it on Meshtastic's `PRIVATE_APP` port (protocol §7.2). Pairing, reconnecting by itself and freeing a left group's channel work as for MeshCore; the radio card names the kind of radio, and Meshtastic chat joins MeshCore's in the chat tab of other radios. The airtime budget counts each packet at the Meshtastic radio's own LoRa settings. Written without Meshtastic's GPL libraries, with a protobuf codec of Tesel's own, on one radio contract, `packages/radio`, which both kinds share ([ADR-0098](docs/decisions/0098-meshtastic-radios.md)). The names and positions of Meshtastic nodes heard are available to the app, not shown yet.
- Connecting a radio asks for its PIN inside the connection, by reading what the radio protects, where Chrome on Android never asked; a connection that fails says why within seconds, and a radio not paired yet shows the three steps to pair it in the system's settings ([ADR-0090](docs/decisions/0090-pairing-a-radio-from-the-app.md)).
- The top bar names what the device reaches its group through — WiFi, 4G/5G, LoRa, several at once — with a green, orange or red dot, and opens a panel with one card per connection. Browsers that do not say whether they use Wi-Fi or mobile data show "WiFi/5G".

### Deployment

- Server image with the relay, the landing page and the web app, for amd64 and arm64 ([ADR-0017](docs/decisions/0017-server-serves-site-and-app.md)).
- One-command, hardened, self-updating VPS install ([ADR-0013](docs/decisions/0013-self-updating-vps.md)). The server runs only images signed by the project's CI, checks each update and goes back to the previous image if it does not start; CI actions and base images are pinned; the database and certificates are backed up every night, off the host if configured; failures of updates, backups and map builds can raise an alert ([ADR-0049](docs/decisions/0049-signed-images-and-checked-updates.md)).
- How much the site and the app are used, counted per day and anonymously: page views, visitors, the sites visits come from, connections, active groups and stored messages, with no address, browser or group kept; shown on a private page, `/stats`, behind a password the installer draws ([ADR-0117](docs/decisions/0117-anonymous-usage-figures.md)).
- Every server setting written in the install's `.env` reaches the server; a setting left empty takes its default. The `.env` is readable by root only, and the guide asks for an A record only (no AAAA).
- `database.sh` copies and restores the server's database; a restored copy always gets a new store identity, so devices send it their events again. An update keeps a copy of the database from before the new version, and going back across a change of the database's schema restores it instead of leaving a server that cannot start. Backups and these copies are refused, with an alert, when they would leave less than `TESEL_MIN_FREE_DISK_MB` free. CI moves `latest` only to the newest commit of main ([ADR-0068](docs/decisions/0068-restorable-copies-and-going-back.md)).
- Security headers: a Content-Security-Policy for the app and the site, also built into the app's page, and a Permissions-Policy.
- The server holds up under load and failure: the backlog goes to a joining device page by page, connections, new groups and pushes are limited per address and in all (`TESEL_PUSHES_PER_IP_PER_SECOND`, 100 by default), an envelope relayed to a group is encoded once for all its members, the weather outlines download is capped at 40 MB and not repeated after a restart that interrupted it, only hashed assets that exist are cached for good, a full disk refuses pushes instead of crashing, storage errors are answered rather than fatal, and `healthz` checks the database. Each server's store has an identity, so devices notice a server restored from a backup and send it their events again ([ADR-0045](docs/decisions/0045-server-keeps-every-distinct-envelope.md), [ADR-0046](docs/decisions/0046-store-identity.md)).

- What an audit of the radio's way back and of the SOS alarm found, put right ([ADR-0104](docs/decisions/0104-sos-across-drops-and-groups.md)): the group is linked to the radio each time it is ready, and a link that failed, with the radio busy, is tried again by itself instead of leaving the radio ready and carrying nothing; a radio that keeps dropping is opened again after longer and longer pauses, and opening one has two minutes in all; a MeshCore radio's messages are read after a drop that cut a read short, so an SOS does not wait in it; an own SOS keeps repeating by radio on its group's channel after another group is opened, with a warning first that it then goes only by radio; and an SOS heard by radio for a group that is not open, or while the group opens, sounds the alarm with the group's name and a way to open it, each call once, even across a reload.
- What an audit of the relay and its deployment found, put right ([ADR-0107](docs/decisions/0107-relay-holds-against-floods.md), [ADR-0108](docs/decisions/0108-deployment-holds-against-floods.md)): the server's pushes are shared out fairly among addresses and groups, and groups in their first day take at most half, so a few addresses can no longer stop every other group's pushes; a push counts against no limit unless all let it through; one address stores at most 512 MB a day and a group 256 MB in its first day (`TESEL_MB_PER_IP_PER_DAY`, `TESEL_MB_PER_GROUP_FIRST_DAY`); a long backlog no longer holds up the server, going in turns, page by page, each once the last has left; joins and messages per connection are limited, and a connection that keeps sending bad messages is closed; what clients leave unread is capped at 96 MB in all (`TESEL_UNREAD_MB`), sized to the container; the retention sweep looks only at the groups it emptied; a push is `stored` only once on the disk; only loopback is a trusted proxy unless set. On the VPS, the server gets only its own settings from `.env` and trusts Caddy on a network of their own, a backup leaves twice the server's disk margin and deletes expired copies first, keeping only the latest on the host once copies go off it, `database.sh` no longer changes the folder of the file it is given, and `database.sh groups` and `purge` find and remove a group's junk. Servers installed before take these files when their installer runs again, which they should do at once.
- What an audit of how the server image is built, published and installed found, put right ([ADR-0109](docs/decisions/0109-build-and-updates-resist-tampering.md)): pull requests build and test the image without any right to publish or sign it, which only a job for a green push to `main` has; BuildKit and QEMU, which run in that job, are pinned by version and digest; each signature carries the number of the CI run, and a server refuses a `latest` from an older run than the one it runs, with an alert, unless the operator chose that image; Dependabot also proposes npm packages and the Node and Caddy images, a week after their release and grouped, and CI fails on high or critical advisories in what ships; pnpm is pinned by hash and installed with Corepack in the image; a dependency with an install script fails the install until it is allowed or ignored by name; the image is built without `.env` files, databases or maps from the working copy; and the guide installs a fixed version by commit, not by a tag that does not exist. Servers take the new update check when their installer runs again.
- Members who go by the same name are told apart on every device: the first to take it keeps it, the others show as "Marta ②", "Marta ③" ([ADR-0096](docs/decisions/0096-same-names-told-apart.md)), and so are groups.
- What an audit of how the app can be deceived found, put right ([ADR-0110](docs/decisions/0110-deception-and-loose-ends.md)): the join dialog says which server a group uses and warns, framed, of a different group with the same name as one of the device's; a device that shares its position asks before sharing it in a group it joins, and pauses otherwise; names are compared by what they look like, the same in every browser, so a zero-width space, a Cyrillic letter, an accent typed apart or a double space no longer gets round the numbering; a name with a character no one sees is refused, and what others write is shown without one, so a right-to-left override turns nothing round; an invitation's server may hold no user, query or fragment, and one on the user's own device is refused unless the app runs there too; a join link that a query or a slash was added to opens, and one without a readable token says it is damaged; a shape file is read in time in proportion to its size, whatever its tags, entities or nesting, and one that cannot be read says so; an app left open asks for a new version every hour and when shown again, and a window kept on its version loads ahead what it loads on first use; and a long word breaks rather than widen a card past the screen.
- What an audit of the radio, the group's state and its sync left, put right ([ADR-0111](docs/decisions/0111-airtime-kept-numbers-per-group.md)): the radio's airtime of the last hour is kept on the device, so a reload no longer lets it spend the urgent 10 % twice in an hour, and one window of the app at a time drives a radio, another one saying it is in use there; a newer version in another window waits for the databases while an own SOS is open or a radio linked; each group numbers a device's events on its own, so its members no longer see the numbers jump by what the device writes in other groups, starting above every number used before; an answer ends an own SOS's repeats only in its own group, and SOS notifications of two groups no longer replace each other; a Meshtastic link keeps at most 500 nodes and no position off the Earth, and a failing listener no longer drops it; an old link let go late no longer drops a newer one to the same radio; who walks a strip and the order of SOS calls are the same on every device when events share a second; and a live event, or a group of hours of positions, is applied in a fraction of the time.

### Known limits

- Phones must keep the app open to share their position: no background position until the Android and iOS apps (ADR-0016). Alarm notifications reach a phone with the app in the background where the browser allows it.
- iPhones cannot pair a radio from Safari: they pair one from the Bluefy browser, or use the internet and reach the radios through any member who bridges both.
- Members are trusted: the app does not prove who wrote an event, so a member could write as another ([ADR-0052](docs/decisions/0052-authorship-is-not-proven.md)). A forged event cannot hide an SOS, and the app says when someone writes under another's name, but a forged chat line, marker or task can take the place of the real one ([ADR-0103](docs/decisions/0103-rivals-full-storage-and-urgent-sync.md)).
- Over LoRa, as over the internet, members can impersonate each other. A radio holds the group secret as its channel key, so whoever reads it out of a radio, or of the radio's app, holds the whole group ([ADR-0099](docs/decisions/0099-sealed-radio-frames.md)).
- Meshtastic and MeshCore radios do not hear each other: a group with both needs a member online to join them. Tesel events use Meshtastic's shared `PRIVATE_APP` port.
- MeshCore `data_type` 0xFF4E is in the development range until registered.
- Radios have been tested in software only, Meshtastic ones without any real radio yet; field tests are pending ([`docs/prueba-de-campo.md`](docs/prueba-de-campo.md)).
