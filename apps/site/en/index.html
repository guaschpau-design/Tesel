Why Tesel

Four simple ideas.

A team in the field needs to know three things: where its people are, what is happening and what to do next. Tesel puts all three on a map everyone sees, and does it like this:

  • For anyone

    Free, with no sign-up. It opens in the browser, you type a name and that is it. No email, no phone number, no password.

  • Understood at once

    Made to be used in a hurry: in a couple of minutes you know how it works, with no manual or training.

  • More than one way

    It uses the internet when there is one and radio when there is not. What you write offline goes out as soon as there is a way.

  • Only the team sees it

    Everything is encrypted with a key only the group has. Nobody else can read it, us included.

Resilient communications

If coverage fails, it carries on by radio.

Normally Tesel goes over the internet, like any app. But the phone can also carry a small pocket radio, paired over Bluetooth. When there is no coverage, messages travel by that radio, hopping from one to the next until they reach the whole team. Nobody has to touch anything: Tesel takes the way that works.

  1. With coverage

    Over wifi or mobile data, like any messaging app. A server passes the messages on to the team, encrypted: it cannot read them.

  2. Without coverage

    Each phone talks to its radio, and the radios pass the messages on to each other, from one to the next, kilometres apart.

  3. Both at once

    A phone that has coverage and a radio acts as a bridge, with nothing to set up: what it hears by radio it sends up to the internet, and what comes from the internet it repeats by radio. So nobody is left out.

  • Everything travels by radio: SOS calls, messages, positions, marks on the map, tasks and reports.
  • The urgent first: radio carries little data, so an SOS goes ahead of messages, and messages ahead of positions.
  • The more radios, the further: each one repeats what it hears. About a kilometre in a city; tens, between high points.
  • No licences, no fees: the radios use a free band (868 MHz in Europe) and very little battery.

What it does

The whole team sees the same thing.

The same map on every phone, at once: where everyone is, what is happening and what has been decided.

  • The team on the map

    Where everyone is, their state and what they carry. Each person decides when to share where they are.

  • SOS with acknowledgement

    A call for help reaches the whole team with its position. Whoever calls sees who has received it, and the others can be guided there.

  • Chat and reports

    Short messages, dictated too, and situation reports with the casualties, the hazards and what is needed.

  • Tasks and sweeps

    Jobs for people or teams, with their place on the map, and search sweeps split into strips.

  • The ground marked

    Sectors, meeting points, routes and danger areas that warn whoever walks into them.

  • Offline maps

    The map of the area, downloaded before setting out, to see it in detail offline.

Who it is for

For teams working in the field.

  • Civil protection

    Volunteer groups, in emergencies and on standby at events.

  • Search and rescue

    Sweeps by sector, with the ground shared out and every team in view.

  • NGOs and humanitarian aid

    Teams deployed where infrastructure fails or does not exist.

  • Events

    Security, medics and organisers at races, festivals and gatherings.

  • Mountains and expeditions

    Groups out of coverage who need to know about each other.

Start now

Up and running in a minute.

It works in the browser of any phone, tablet or computer. No app stores, and it updates itself.

  1. Open Tesel

    Go to tesel.eu/app and type your name. To keep it at hand, add it to your home screen from the browser's menu.

  2. Create the group

    The coordinator creates it and invites the rest with a QR code or a link. Whoever opens it is in.

  3. Get ready to lose the network

    Download the map of your area on wifi and, if you have radios, pair them with your phones. And practise on a quiet day.

    Only the group holds the key. The server cannot read anything.

Questions

Before you start.

Does it cost anything?

No. Tesel is free and needs no sign-up. Only the radios, if you use them, are bought.

Do we need radios?

No. With coverage, Tesel needs nothing else. Radios are plan B for when the network fails: best to have them ready beforehand.

Does everyone need a radio?

No. As long as some carry one, they act as bridges for the rest. Mind you: whoever has neither a radio nor coverage hears nothing until they get one of the two back.

Which radios work?

LoRa radios with the MeshCore firmware (“Companion Bluetooth”). We will publish the models we recommend here.

Does it work on an iPhone?

Yes. To pair a radio over Bluetooth on an iPhone, open Tesel in the Bluefy browser, as Safari does not allow it.

Do we have to install anything?

No. It opens in the browser and is always up to date. If you want it like any other app, add it to the home screen from the browser's menu.

Can we see the map offline?

Yes, if you downloaded it beforehand. In Tesel you choose the area and save it on the phone over wifi; afterwards it shows in full detail even with no network.

Who can get into our group?

Only whoever receives the coordinator's invitation: a QR code or a link. Without it, nobody can see the group or what happens in it.
